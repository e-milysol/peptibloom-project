# Retatrutide Evidence Inventory v0.1

Status: WORKING EVIDENCE INVENTORY / NOT PUBLICATION-READY
Evidence cutoff: 2026-09-03
Pilot: PB-SCI-PILOT-001

## Scope

This inventory records source-level evidence for retatrutide (LY3437943) before editorial drafting. It distinguishes peer-reviewed primary evidence from trial registry records, company topline communications, secondary reviews and anecdotal material.

Nothing in this file authorizes clinical use, dosing advice, product claims or publication.

## Source classes

- P1 = peer-reviewed primary human clinical study.
- P2 = peer-reviewed primary mechanistic/preclinical study.
- R1 = official clinical trial registry record.
- C1 = sponsor/company communication or conference/topline disclosure; not equivalent to a peer-reviewed full primary publication.
- S1 = secondary scientific review.
- A1 = anecdotal/community source; none accepted into v0.1.

## Core peer-reviewed human evidence

### E-001 — Phase 1b / multiple ascending dose / type 2 diabetes

Source class: P1
Citation: Urva S, Coskun T, Loh MT, et al. LY3437943, a novel triple GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptor agonist in people with type 2 diabetes: a phase 1b, multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomised, multiple-ascending dose trial. Lancet. 2022;400(10366):1869-1881. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(22)02033-5. PMID:36354040.
Trial: NCT04143802.
Sponsor/funding: Eli Lilly and Company.
Population: 72 adults with type 2 diabetes; 12-week study.
Key evidence:
- Once-weekly subcutaneous administration was studied.
- Pharmacokinetics were approximately dose proportional.
- Reported half-life was approximately 6 days.
- Glucose and body-weight reductions were observed, greatest in higher-dose cohorts.
- Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most frequently reported treatment-emergent adverse events.
Important limitations:
- Early phase, small cohorts, short duration.
- High sponsor involvement; many authors were Lilly employees/shareholders.
- Not suitable alone to establish long-term efficacy, long-term safety or population-wide clinical recommendations.

### E-002 — Phase 2 / type 2 diabetes

Source class: P1
Citation: Rosenstock J, Frias J, Jastreboff AM, et al. Retatrutide, a GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist, for people with type 2 diabetes: a randomised, double-blind, placebo and active-controlled, parallel-group, phase 2 trial conducted in the USA. Lancet. 2023;402(10401):529-544. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01053-X. PMID:37385280.
Trial: NCT04867785.
Sponsor/funding: Eli Lilly and Company.
Population: 281 randomized adults with T2D; 36-week study.
Comparators: placebo and dulaglutide 1.5 mg.
Key evidence:
- HbA1c reductions at 24 weeks were dose dependent and statistically superior to placebo for most clinically developed doses.
- At 36 weeks, mean body-weight change ranged to approximately -16.9% in the highest studied dose group, versus -3.0% placebo and -2.0% dulaglutide.
- GI adverse events were common, usually mild-to-moderate.
- No severe hypoglycaemia and no deaths were reported in this trial.
Important limitations:
- Phase 2, 36 weeks.
- Dulaglutide 1.5 mg is not a definitive active comparator for the strongest currently available incretin-based therapy.
- Sponsor involvement and declared financial relationships are substantial and must remain visible in public provenance.

### E-003 — Phase 2 / obesity without diabetes

Source class: P1
Citation: Jastreboff AM, Kaplan LM, Frías JP, et al. Triple-Hormone-Receptor Agonist Retatrutide for Obesity — A Phase 2 Trial. N Engl J Med. 2023;389(6):514-526. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2301972. PMID:37366315.
Trial: NCT04881760.
Sponsor/funding: Eli Lilly and Company.
Population: 338 adults with obesity, or overweight plus a weight-related condition; 48 weeks.
Key evidence:
- Mean percentage body-weight change at 48 weeks: -8.7% (1 mg), -17.1% (combined 4 mg), -22.8% (combined 8 mg), -24.2% (12 mg), versus -2.1% placebo.
- GI adverse events were the most common and were dose-related; lower starting dose reduced tolerability burden.
- Dose-dependent heart-rate increases peaked around week 24 and declined thereafter.
- Serious adverse events occurred at similar overall frequency in retatrutide and placebo groups (4% each in the report).
- One pancreatitis serious adverse event was reported; asymptomatic amylase/lipase elevations occurred.
- Mild-to-moderate altered/enhanced skin sensation was reported more often with retatrutide than placebo.
Important limitations:
- Phase 2 and 48 weeks only.
- Majority White and US-only population; limited generalisability.
- The weight-loss trajectory had not clearly plateaued at 48 weeks, so durability after longer treatment and after withdrawal was not established by this trial.

### E-004 — Phase 2a MASLD substudy

Source class: P1
Citation: Sanyal AJ, Kaplan LM, Frias JP, et al. Triple hormone receptor agonist retatrutide for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease: a randomized phase 2a trial. Nat Med. 2024;30(7):2037-2048. doi:10.1038/s41591-024-03018-2. PMID:38858523.
Trial: NCT04881760 substudy.
Sponsor/funding context: multiple Lilly-affiliated authors; sponsor involvement must remain visible.
Population: 98 participants from the obesity study with liver fat >=10%.
Key evidence:
- Mean relative liver-fat change at 24 weeks was approximately -42.9%, -57.0%, -81.4% and -82.4% for 1, 4, 8 and 12 mg respectively, versus +0.3% placebo.
- Normal liver fat (<5%) at week 24 occurred in 27%, 52%, 79% and 86% across increasing retatrutide doses versus 0% placebo.
Important limitations:
- Small substudy.
- Imaging/liver-fat outcome is not equivalent to proven reduction in fibrosis progression, liver-related morbidity or mortality.
- 48-week MRI sample was substantially smaller than at week 24.

### E-005 — Phase 3 / TRANSCEND-T2D-1

Source class: P1
Citation: Bajaj HS, Welch M, Shah P, et al. Efficacy and safety of retatrutide, a GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptor agonist, in people with type 2 diabetes and inadequate glycaemic control with diet and exercise (TRANSCEND-T2D-1): a double-blind, randomised, phase 3 trial. Lancet. 2026;407(10546):2402-2413. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00967-0. PMID:42250575.
Trial: NCT06354660.
Sponsor/funding: Eli Lilly and Company.
Population: 537 randomized adults with T2D inadequately controlled by diet and exercise alone; 40 weeks.
Arms: retatrutide 4 mg, 9 mg, 12 mg, placebo; once-weekly subcutaneous injection.
Key evidence:
- Mean HbA1c change at week 40: -1.69% (4 mg), -1.86% (9 mg), -1.94% (12 mg) versus -0.81% placebo; all treatment differences versus placebo p<0.0001.
- Mean percentage body-weight change: -11.5%, -13.9%, -15.3% versus -2.6% placebo using the treatment-regimen estimand reported in the peer-reviewed abstract.
- GI adverse events were most frequent and generally mild-to-moderate.
- Discontinuation due to adverse events: 2-5% retatrutide, 0% placebo.
- No severe hypoglycaemia was reported.
- Two deaths occurred in the 4 mg arm and were assessed as unrelated to study drug.
Important limitations:
- 40-week monotherapy population with relatively short mean diabetes duration; cannot be generalized automatically to all T2D populations or combination regimens.
- Sponsor-funded with multiple sponsor-employed authors/shareholders.

## Mechanistic / structural evidence

### E-006 — Triple receptor structural pharmacology

Source class: P2
Citation: Structural insights into the triple agonism at GLP-1R, GIPR and GCGR manifested by retatrutide. Cell Discovery. 2024. PMID:39019866.
Key evidence:
- Cryo-EM structures demonstrate direct binding/activation architecture at GLP-1R, GIPR and GCGR.
- Published functional comparisons report greater relative potency at human GIPR than native GIP and lower relative potency at human GLP-1R and GCGR than their native ligands.
Boundary:
- Receptor potency and structural engagement support mechanism; they do not by themselves prove clinical efficacy or safety.

## Sponsor communications tracked separately

### C-001 — TRIUMPH-1 topline / May-June 2026
Source class: C1
Sponsor-reported result includes mean weight loss of 28.3% at 80 weeks for 12 mg in TRIUMPH-1 and additional OA/OSA endpoints.
Rule: may be described as sponsor-reported/topline or conference-presented only; must not be phrased as equivalent to a peer-reviewed primary publication.

### C-002 — TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 topline / 2026-07-23
Source class: C1
Sponsor-reported results include weight loss up to 20.8% at 80 weeks in TRIUMPH-2 and 22.6% at 80 weeks in TRIUMPH-3.
Rule: detailed results were stated by Lilly to be intended for later meeting presentation/publication. Until independently reviewed, do not upgrade to P1.

## Current evidence-level summary

- Triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonism: strong mechanistic support.
- Glycaemic efficacy in T2D: strong short-to-medium-term human evidence, now including one phase 3 peer-reviewed trial.
- Weight reduction in obesity: strong phase 2 evidence; phase 3 sponsor-reported findings are highly relevant but not yet equivalent to peer-reviewed full primary data in this inventory.
- Liver-fat reduction in MASLD: promising phase 2a evidence; hard liver outcomes/fibrosis benefit not established.
- Long-term cardiovascular/renal outcomes: unresolved.
- Long-term safety and rare adverse events: unresolved/incomplete.
- Approved clinical dosing: none at cutoff because retatrutide remains investigational.
- Third-party vial reconstitution/stability protocols: unsupported for publication as evidence-based clinical guidance.
