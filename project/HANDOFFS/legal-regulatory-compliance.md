## Primary-source framework record — discovery, not final approval

Research currentness checked: 2026-08-19.

- EU / GDPR: Regulation (EU) 2016/679, EUR-Lex consolidated text. Scope used here: controller definition; processing principles/storage limitation; Article 6 lawful bases; Articles 11–13 rights/transparency; Article 28 processors; Article 32 security; Chapter V international transfers.
- Spain / audience measurement: AEPD, `Guía sobre el uso de cookies para herramientas de medición de audiencia`, v. January 2024. Scope used here: conditions and safeguards for the narrow Article 22.2 LSSI consent exemption for audience-measurement cookies/similar technologies.

Key discovery findings:

1. GDPR defines the controller by determination of purposes and means; a future incorporated entity is not required merely to state that the current controller fact is unresolved.
2. GDPR requires a lawful basis for processing personal data; the basis must be selected against the actual processing, not assigned globally to 'analytics'.
3. GDPR transparency requires controller identity/contact, purposes/legal basis, recipients and transfer information where applicable, and retention period/criteria, among other information.
4. Processor use requires sufficient guarantees and an Article 28-compliant binding arrangement; subprocessors are subject to the Article 28 chain.
5. Restricted third-country transfers must comply with GDPR Chapter V; the actual transfer path must be established before selecting/validating a mechanism.
6. Storage limitation requires necessity-based retention. The AEPD exempt-audience model states a 13-month tracker lifetime and maximum 25-month retention for collected information, subject to periodic necessity review; these are not automatic PeptiBloom targets.
7. The AEPD exemption is narrow: measurement must be strictly limited to exclusive audience measurement for the publisher and anonymous statistics; no cross-processing, third-party transmission, cross-site tracking or provider reuse inconsistent with the exemption. Provider use also requires Article 28 terms, no reuse, restricted purposes and compliant transfers, plus documented configuration assessment.
8. AEPD requires users to be informed about exempt audience-measurement technologies. Consent exemption therefore does not mean transparency exemption.
9. GDPR Article 11 supports a non-identifying rights model: where purposes do not require identification, the controller need not acquire additional information solely to identify a person for GDPR compliance; operational handling still requires a clear rights/contact process.
10. GDPR Article 32 makes security risk-based and shared between controller/processor. Legal needs Security Assurance plus implementing-domain evidence of actual controls before closing applicable Legal/Privacy gates.
