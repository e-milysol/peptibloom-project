## Copy-edit decisions

### Keep

- “GLP-3” correction: useful because it addresses a common public misconception.
- distinction between peptide backbone and complete modified chemistry;
- explicit trial-regimen versus approved-dose distinction;
- refusal to invent reconstitution/stability guidance;
- direct comparison questions for tirzepatide and semaglutide;
- MASLD surrogate-outcome caveat;
- cardiovascular/renal outcome caveat;
- common-errors section.

### Consolidate

The draft repeats “not approved / investigational” and “trial regimen is not a recommendation” in several sections. Keep the warning at first relevant occurrence and then use shorter cross-references where possible. Repetition should be retained only where removing it could create a safety or interpretation error when a section is read independently.

### Strengthen clarity without strengthening claims

Prefer:
- “In the studied population…”
- “The trial reported…”
- “At the 2026-09-03 evidence cutoff…”
- “This remains unresolved…”

Avoid:
- “Retatrutide causes…” when the evidence supports only an observed trial effect;
- “safe” without scope/time qualifier;
- “best”, “superior”, “most powerful”, “breakthrough” or other commercial superlatives;
- “recommended dose”, “optimal dose”, “cycle” or “stack” unless future approved Evidence explicitly supports the exact claim.

## Section-specific editorial notes

### Identity / chemistry

Keep formula, molecular weight and sequence together visually. Add a small “identity note” explaining that the one-letter sequence is a backbone representation and does not encode all modifications. This avoids presenting a chemically incomplete sequence as the whole molecule.

### Mechanism

Use a three-receptor visual with GIPR, GLP-1R and GCGR converging on one molecule. The visual must communicate receptor targets only; it must not assign unproven percentages of clinical effect to each receptor.

### Pharmacokinetics

Present the approximately six-day half-life as an early-human-study observation. The nearby once-weekly fact should be labelled “investigational study schedule”, not “frequency recommendation”.

### Stability / conservation / reconstitution

These sections should not look unfinished. Use an explicit Evidence Gap treatment:

“PeptiBloom did not identify formulation-specific evidence sufficient to establish a universal third-party-vial instruction.”

Then explain why formulation, excipients, concentration, container closure, pH, temperature and microbiological controls matter. The absence of a protocol is itself an evidence finding.

### Dosage / frequency / duration

Rename the visual chapter to “Studied regimens” rather than “How to use”. Trial numbers can be shown only with trial name/population/duration context. Do not provide syringe-unit conversions or a simplified take-home regimen.

### Combinations / synergies / interactions

Separate molecular multi-receptor agonism from combination therapy. This is a particularly important misconception-prevention point.

### Safety

Use two visual groups:
- commonly observed tolerability signals in the reviewed trials;
- unresolved/long-term safety questions.

Do not create an apparent official contraindication box because no approved retatrutide label exists at cutoff.

### FAQ

Keep the FAQ because it directly addresses misinformation. Answers should be concise and link/read through to the detailed evidence section in Web implementation.

### Common errors

Retain all ten items. This section is highly aligned with PeptiBloom’s “know what we know / know what we don’t” identity.

## Recomendación PeptiBloom — editorial specification

The section should be renamed visually as “Recomendación PeptiBloom — interpretación de la evidencia” or equivalent localized wording so it cannot be mistaken for a treatment recommendation.

Recommended structure:

1. What the evidence supports.
2. What increases confidence.
3. What limits confidence.
4. What PeptiBloom will not infer.
5. Bottom-line evidence interpretation.

It must not contain an administration recommendation.

## Proposed Golden Master opening summary

Retatrutide is an investigational peptide engineered to activate GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Peer-reviewed human trials support substantial effects on glycaemic control and body weight in the populations and durations studied, including phase 3 evidence in type 2 diabetes. Evidence is less mature for long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes, rare-event safety, direct superiority over established incretin therapies and MASLD outcomes beyond liver-fat imaging. At the evidence cutoff, retatrutide is not an approved medicine, and the reviewed evidence does not establish a universal third-party-vial reconstitution, storage or self-directed dosing protocol.

11. ❓ Open questions — CV/renal outcomes, comparative trials, rare-event safety.

A4 and vertical-social versions must preserve the same qualifiers. The vertical version may shorten text but may not drop uncertainty labels to gain space.
