## Evidence at a glance

Retatrutide is an investigational peptide engineered to activate the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Peer-reviewed human trials support substantial effects on glycaemic control and body weight in the populations and durations studied, including phase 3 evidence in type 2 diabetes. Evidence is less mature for long-term cardiovascular and renal outcomes, rare-event safety, direct superiority over established incretin therapies and MASLD outcomes beyond liver-fat imaging. At the evidence cutoff, retatrutide is not an approved medicine, and the reviewed evidence does not establish a universal third-party-vial reconstitution, storage or self-directed dosing protocol.

# A. Identity and chemistry

## 1. Name

**Retatrutide**

Development designation: **LY3437943 / LY-3437943**.

FDA GSRS substance identifier: **UNII NOP2Y096GV**. A UNII identifies a substance; it does not indicate regulatory approval.

## 2. Other names

- LY3437943
- LY-3437943
- Retatrutide [INN/USAN]

“GLP-3” is sometimes used informally online but is not an appropriate scientific classification. Retatrutide is a single triple **GIPR/GLP-1R/GCGR agonist**.

## 3. Formula

FDA GSRS alternative chemical representation for the fully modified neutral molecule: **C221H342N46O68**.

Identity note: the GSRS protein representation also provides backbone-derived calculations. Those should not be confused with the chemically modified molecule or with a particular salt/form supplied by a manufacturer.

## 4. Molecular weight

Approximately **4,731.36 Da** for the fully modified neutral molecule under the FDA GSRS alternative chemical representation.

This value should not be transferred without qualification to a different salt, formulation or vendor-defined material.

## 5. Amino-acid sequence and structural modifications

Retatrutide is a modified **39-residue** peptide based on a GIP-like backbone.

FDA GSRS backbone representation:

`YAQGTFTSDYSILLDKKAQAAFIEYLLEGGPSSGAPPPS`

This natural-letter sequence is not a complete chemical description. The substance contains non-standard residues and lipidation/linker chemistry, including Aib substitutions, an alpha-methyl leucine residue, a C20 fatty-diacid moiety attached through a gamma-glutamyl/AEEA linker at lysine, and a C-terminal amide.

**PeptiBloom interpretation:** sequence and complete chemical identity must be distinguished because the modifications materially affect the molecule’s properties and pharmacokinetics.

## 6. Family

Retatrutide is an investigational multi-receptor metabolic peptide agonist targeting:

- glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (**GIPR**);
- glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (**GLP-1R**);
- glucagon receptor (**GCGR**).

**Evidence:** ✅ Solid for receptor identity/mechanism.

# B. Development and pharmacology

## 7. History

Clinical development was initially reported under LY3437943. A phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study in adults with type 2 diabetes was published in 2022. Phase 2 trials in type 2 diabetes and obesity followed in 2023, and a MASLD liver-fat substudy was published in 2024. In 2026, TRANSCEND-T2D-1 added peer-reviewed phase 3 evidence in type 2 diabetes.

At the cutoff, the development programme continues to investigate obesity, type 2 diabetes, direct comparisons with established incretin therapies, cardiovascular/renal outcomes and associated metabolic conditions.

Regulatory status at **2026-09-03:** investigational; no approved retatrutide prescribing label was identified in the accepted corpus.

## 8. Mechanism of action

Retatrutide is engineered to activate **GIPR, GLP-1R and GCGR** within one molecule. Structural and functional work supports engagement of all three receptors.

The rationale combines incretin pharmacology involved in glycaemic regulation and appetite/energy intake with glucagon-receptor pharmacology that may contribute additional metabolic effects.

⚠ **Important boundary:** demonstrating receptor engagement does not prove how much of the clinical weight or glycaemic effect is attributable to each receptor individually. Mechanistic plausibility must not be converted into an unsupported quantitative human claim.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics

The phase 1b human study reported approximately dose-proportional pharmacokinetics and an elimination half-life of roughly **6 days**.

This is consistent with the **once-weekly investigational administration schedule** used in the clinical programme.

⚠ The estimate comes from early-phase human testing and is not approved prescribing information.

# C. Handling and preparation evidence

## 10. Stability

❓ **Evidence gap.** The reviewed evidence does not establish a universal post-reconstitution stability period for third-party lyophilised retatrutide vials.

Stability is formulation-specific and can depend on chemical form, excipients, concentration, pH, container closure, manufacturing controls, temperature, light exposure and microbiological conditions.

A statement such as “stable for X days after reconstitution” therefore requires formulation-specific analytical evidence; PeptiBloom will not infer one from unrelated products or vendor convention.

## 11. Conservation / storage

❓ **Evidence gap.** No universal storage instruction for arbitrary third-party retatrutide material is authorized by this review.

Three categories must remain distinct:

- investigational clinical-supply handling;
- manufacturer-specific research-material instructions;
- validated pharmaceutical stability data.

A recommendation belonging to one category cannot automatically be generalized to the others.

## 12. Reconstitution

❓ **Universal validated protocol not established.**

The clinical literature describes investigational subcutaneous administration but does not establish a general bacteriostatic-water recipe for third-party lyophilised vials.

**PeptiBloom position:** an evidence gap is more informative than a fabricated protocol. No reconstitution volume, syringe-unit conversion or post-reconstitution lifetime is provided in this monograph.

# D. Studied regimens and duration

## 13. Dosage — studied regimens, not approved dosing

There is no approved retatrutide dose at the evidence cutoff.

Human trials have investigated multiple once-weekly dose levels and escalation schemes. Examples include phase 2 obesity arms from **1 mg to 12 mg weekly** and TRANSCEND-T2D-1 maintenance arms of **4 mg, 9 mg and 12 mg weekly**.

These values describe protocol-controlled experimental regimens. They are included to interpret the studies, not as a PeptiBloom dosing recommendation.

**Evidence for trial regimens:** ✅ Solid.
**Evidence for an approved public-use dose:** ❓ None at cutoff.

## 14. Frequency — investigational study schedule

Once-weekly subcutaneous administration is used consistently in the clinical programme and is pharmacokinetically plausible in light of the approximately six-day half-life observed in phase 1b.

This describes how retatrutide was studied; it is not an approved frequency recommendation.

## 15. Duration and the concept of a “cycle”

Peer-reviewed trials in the accepted corpus include protocol periods of approximately **12, 36, 40 and 48 weeks**, depending on the study.

❓ A scientifically validated self-directed “cycle” has not been established. Clinical-trial duration should not be translated into bodybuilding/research-market cycling advice.

# E. Combinations, synergies and interactions

## 16. Frequent combinations

No evidence-based PeptiBloom “stacking” protocol is supported.

Some phase 3 programmes deliberately study retatrutide in participants receiving background metabolic therapies. For example, TRANSCEND-T2D-2 compares retatrutide with semaglutide in participants receiving metformin with or without an SGLT2 inhibitor; TRANSCEND-T2D-3 studies a population with renal impairment receiving basal insulin with or without other background therapy.

Those study designs answer research questions; they are not combination recommendations.

## 17. Synergies

The principal demonstrated pharmacologic “combination” is internal to the molecule itself: GIPR + GLP-1R + GCGR agonism.

That multi-receptor design is not equivalent to stacking retatrutide with another peptide or incretin drug.

❓ The accepted evidence does not establish that adding another GLP-1/GIP agonist produces a superior benefit-risk profile.

## 18. Interactions

The complete drug-drug interaction profile remains incompletely mapped in the reviewed corpus.

This is particularly relevant when moving from selected monotherapy populations to regimens involving insulin or other glucose-lowering therapies, where hypoglycaemia and treatment-management questions can differ.

❓ Interaction guidance remains incomplete rather than inferred.

# F. Safety and monitoring

## 19. Possible adverse effects

### Recurrently observed tolerability signals

Across the reviewed human trials, gastrointestinal events were the most consistently reported adverse effects, including **nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and constipation**. They were generally described as mild to moderate, although adverse events led some participants to discontinue treatment.

### Other observations requiring context

- transient/dose-related heart-rate increases were described in the phase 2 obesity study;
- one serious acute pancreatitis event occurred in that trial;
- asymptomatic pancreatic-enzyme elevations were observed;
- altered/enhanced skin sensation or hyperesthesia was reported in a minority of participants;
- adverse-event-related discontinuations also occurred in TRANSCEND-T2D-1.

The pancreatitis observation does not establish its long-term incidence, but it also prevents a claim that pancreatitis risk is absent.

### What remains unresolved

Long-term rare-event safety is incompletely characterized. Absence of severe hypoglycaemia in selected monotherapy trials does not prove absence of hypoglycaemia risk under insulin-containing or other combination regimens.

## 20. Contraindications

No approved retatrutide prescribing label exists at the cutoff, so PeptiBloom does not create an official contraindication list by copying one from another incretin medicine.

Important evidence gaps remain for pregnancy/lactation, paediatric use, several special populations and some combination settings.

**Current scientific representation:** not established / investigational.

## 21. Parameters studied for monitoring

Clinical trials monitor domains such as body weight, HbA1c/glucose, adverse events, vital signs and laboratory measures relevant to their objectives.

These observations tell us what investigators measured. They do not establish a universal PeptiBloom monitoring prescription for unsupervised use.

# G. Interpretation and reader guidance

## 22. Frequently asked questions

### Is retatrutide approved?

No at the **2026-09-03** evidence cutoff. It remains investigational in the accepted corpus.

### Is retatrutide simply a “stronger GLP-1”?

No. That description misses two receptor targets. Retatrutide is a single triple GIPR/GLP-1R/GCGR agonist.

### Has it produced large weight reductions in human trials?

Yes, within studied populations and durations. In the peer-reviewed phase 2 obesity trial, the 12 mg study arm reported a mean **24.2% reduction from baseline at 48 weeks**, compared with **2.1% with placebo**. This is a group-level trial result, not an individual guarantee.

### Is it proven better than tirzepatide?

❓ Not at the cutoff. TRIUMPH-5 is designed as a direct phase 3 comparison, but accepted comparative results are not available in this review.

### Is it proven better than semaglutide in type 2 diabetes?

❓ Not at the cutoff. TRANSCEND-T2D-2 addresses that comparison; accepted results are not yet available in this review.

### Does it reverse fatty liver disease?

⚠ A small phase 2a MASLD substudy demonstrated large MRI-measured reductions in liver fat. That is not equivalent to demonstrated fibrosis reversal, prevention of cirrhosis, reduced liver-related mortality or established long-term disease modification.

### Can PeptiBloom provide a universal reconstitution schedule?

No from the current evidence. Formulation-specific evidence sufficient to validate a universal third-party-vial protocol was not identified.

### Is long-term cardiovascular protection proven?

❓ No. TRIUMPH-Outcomes is designed to investigate cardiovascular and kidney outcomes; no accepted results were available at the cutoff.

## 23. Ten common interpretation errors

1. Calling retatrutide “GLP-3” as if that were a receptor or accepted pharmacologic class.
2. Treating clinical-trial doses as approved doses.
3. Presenting sponsor topline results as though they were automatically equivalent to peer-reviewed primary evidence.
4. Assuming the magnitude of weight loss in one population and duration applies to every individual.
5. Equating liver-fat reduction with demonstrated fibrosis reversal or prevention of liver events.
6. Treating improvements in risk markers as proof of cardiovascular-event reduction.
7. Claiming superiority over tirzepatide before accepted head-to-head results are available.
8. Copying research-vendor reconstitution/storage directions and calling them clinical evidence.
9. Treating absence of a rare adverse event in a finite trial as proof that the risk does not exist.
10. Presenting preclinical or mechanistic findings as demonstrated human therapeutic outcomes.

## 24. Scientific evidence level

### Human glycaemic and weight efficacy — ✅ Solid within scope

Confidence is supported by randomized human trials, peer-reviewed phase 2 studies and peer-reviewed phase 3 evidence in type 2 diabetes.

The qualifier matters: confidence applies to the studied populations, endpoints and durations, not to every possible population or long-term outcome.

### MASLD — ⚠ Promising but limited

Large liver-fat reductions are supported by a small imaging substudy. Hard liver outcomes and fibrosis reversal are not established.

### Long-term outcomes and rare-event safety — ❓ Unresolved

Cardiovascular/renal event reduction, durability after discontinuation and uncommon long-term adverse outcomes remain incompletely characterized.

### Regulatory / approved-use evidence — ❓ None

Retatrutide remains investigational at the cutoff.

### Third-party preparation and stability — ❓ Insufficient

The reviewed evidence does not justify universal reconstitution, storage-lifetime or syringe-conversion instructions for arbitrary third-party vials.

# I. Recomendación PeptiBloom — interpretación de la evidencia

This section is an evidence interpretation, **not a treatment recommendation**.

## 1. What the evidence supports

Retatrutide has a well-supported triple GIPR/GLP-1R/GCGR pharmacologic identity and a substantial human clinical programme. Peer-reviewed trials demonstrate clinically meaningful glycaemic improvement in studied adults with type 2 diabetes and substantial body-weight reduction in studied populations. The evidence base now includes peer-reviewed phase 3 T2D data.

## 2. What increases confidence

Confidence is strengthened by:

- randomized controlled human studies across successive development phases;
- replication of weight and glycaemic signals across multiple trials;
- a mechanistically coherent receptor profile;
- peer-reviewed phase 3 evidence rather than reliance solely on early-phase or sponsor topline results.

## 3. What limits confidence

Important uncertainties remain:

- long-term cardiovascular and kidney event outcomes;
- rare-event and very-long-term safety;
- durability after treatment discontinuation;
- direct comparative superiority versus tirzepatide and semaglutide in the ongoing relevant programmes;
- special populations not adequately represented in the accepted evidence;
- whether the strong MASLD liver-fat signal translates into hard liver outcomes.

## 4. What PeptiBloom will not infer

PeptiBloom does not infer:

- an approved or “optimal” dose from trial regimens;
- a self-directed titration or cycle;
- a universal third-party-vial reconstitution protocol;
- a post-reconstitution stability period without formulation-specific analytical evidence;
- a stacking recommendation;
- proven cardiovascular/renal protection from surrogate improvements;
- fibrosis reversal from liver-fat reduction;
- comparative superiority before direct results are accepted.

## 5. Bottom line

**PeptiBloom evidence interpretation:** Retatrutide is one of the more scientifically mature investigational multi-receptor metabolic peptides in the current research landscape, with strong short-to-medium-term human evidence for glycaemic and weight outcomes within studied populations. That maturity does not make it an approved medicine and does not close major questions around long-term outcomes, rare-event safety, comparative superiority or third-party preparation. The scientifically defensible position is therefore to describe its human efficacy signal as strong within scope while keeping administration guidance and unresolved outcomes explicitly outside what the evidence currently supports.
