**Workstream:** `PB-AV-001 — Bloom & Eviden Digital Actors`

**Status:** `REVIEW`  
**Source authority:** `PB-EV-BLOOM-CHARACTER-MASTER-V1.0`

**Purpose:** define the immutable identity, controlled variation, visual invariants and drift-prevention rules for Bloom as a reusable digital actor.

---

## 1. Identity summary

**Name:** Bloom  
**Role:** Communicator & Educator / PeptiBloom Ambassador  
**Narrative function:** Connect science with people through clarity, empathy and curiosity. Introduce topics, ask accessible questions, translate complexity without erasing uncertainty, and keep the audience engaged.

**Personality anchors:**
- warm;
- curious;
- positive;
- approachable;
- inspiring;
- people-oriented;
- encouraging rather than authoritative.

**Catchphrase currently present on the approved master sheet:** `Curiosity blooms knowledge.`

This catchphrase is a character-brand element. It is not a scientific claim.

---

## 2. Canonical visual identity

The approved master depicts Bloom as a stylized young-adult female science communicator with the following recurring visual identity:

- apparent age: late 20s to early 30s;
- medium-light / warm skin tone as represented in the canonical master;
- long, voluminous, wavy dark-brown hair;
- side-parted hairstyle with soft face-framing waves;
- large expressive brown eyes;
- soft arched brows;
- small refined nose;
- warm, friendly facial structure;
- natural makeup / soft lip color;
- slim-to-average stylized body proportions;
- white laboratory coat with purple family accents;
- purple/violet inner top;
- dark fitted trousers;
- white sneakers with subtle purple detailing;
- orchid motif/pin as a signature Bloom identity element.

The canonical appearance is stylized illustration / polished character-art, not photorealistic human portraiture.

---

## 3. Immutable facial invariants

The following identity traits must remain recognizably stable across all approved derivatives:

1. Overall face shape and proportions.
2. Brown eye color and large expressive-eye treatment.
3. Dark-brown wavy hair color, density and overall silhouette.
4. Hair length: long, extending below the shoulders.
5. Side-parted styling and soft curls/waves.
6. Eyebrow color and general arch.
7. Friendly, approachable resting expression.
8. Nose and mouth proportions consistent with the canonical master.
9. No dramatic age-up or age-down transformation under v1.0.
10. No identity-changing facial stylization that makes Bloom look like a different person/character.

### Prohibited facial drift

Do not under v1.0:

- change eye color;
- change hair to blonde, red, black-blue, pastel, straight bob, short crop or other identity-changing hairstyle;
- add/remove major facial features;
- change ethnicity-coded appearance through regeneration drift;
- give Bloom an exaggerated glamour, fashion-model, anime-chibi or photoreal face that breaks continuity;
- introduce permanent glasses unless a future approved version explicitly does so;
- materially change apparent age.

---

## 4. Hair invariants

**Canonical:** long, dark-brown, glossy, voluminous, wavy/curled hair with a side part.

Allowed controlled variation:

- natural secondary motion for animation;
- small changes caused by head rotation, gravity and wind-like scene effects;
- minor strand placement variation;
- front/3⁄4/profile/back views as already represented in the approved master.

Not allowed without new approval:

- short hair;
- ponytail/bun as default appearance;
- major recolor;
- radically different curl pattern;
- bangs/fringe that materially alter face recognition;
- hairstyle changes that create a “different Bloom.”

---

## 5. Body and proportion invariants

Bloom's body proportions follow the canonical stylized full-body references.

Maintain:

- the same approximate head-to-body ratio;
- similar shoulder width and body silhouette;
- long-leg stylization consistent with the master;
- balanced, professional stance;
- no exaggerated sexualization;
- no extreme muscular, childlike or fashion-doll proportion changes.

Exact numerical anthropometric ratios are **not currently specified by the source sheet**. Future rigging may derive production measurements, but those measurements must be treated as technical derivatives and must not silently redefine the canonical visual identity.

---

## 6. Wardrobe system

### Canonical default wardrobe

- white lab coat;
- subtle purple piping/accent details;
- purple/violet top beneath the coat;
- dark trousers;
- white sneakers with purple details;
- orchid character motif/pin.

This is the default recognizable Bloom actor wardrobe for scientific/editorial scenes.

### Controlled wardrobe variation

Allowed only when a scene genuinely requires it and continuity remains obvious:

- coat open/closed within canonical styling;
- sleeves/coat movement caused by pose or animation;
- minor practical variants derived from the same wardrobe system;
- approved PeptiBloom event/presentation variants created as explicit derivatives.

Not allowed by default:

- random fashion outfits from generation to generation;
- unrelated brand colors replacing Bloom purple;
- medical scrubs presented as the canonical default;
- high heels, formal gown, casual streetwear or costume changes that materially reduce character recognizability unless separately approved;
- heavy jewelry/accessories that compete with the character system.

## 7. Signature color system

The approved character sheet labels Bloom's signature system as **Bloom Orchid Purple**.

Visible primary swatches on the approved sheet include:

- `#6E3A8A`
- `#6E8BB7` as visually shown on the source sheet; exact future production use must be rechecked against the binary because source typography may be stylized/AI-rendered;
- `#B888D4`
- `#DCC7EA`
- `#F3ECF8`

Visible neutral swatches include dark charcoal/gray through white.

### Color authority rule

The image binary is the visual authority. Text transcribed from the generated sheet is secondary because AI-rendered small text can contain transcription artifacts. Before using any hex value as a production constant, sample/verify it from the canonical asset or a separately approved palette file.

Purple remains Bloom's signature family color. Do not arbitrarily convert Bloom to Neuro blue, Lab teal, Metabolic gold or Blend terracotta.

## 8. Signature symbols and accessories

The approved master associates Bloom with:

- orchid;
- orchid + DNA/molecular visual language;
- tablet;
- notebook;
- research vial in scientific-context scenes;
- pen;
- orchid pin;
- PeptiBloom ID badge.

These are supporting props, not mandatory in every frame.

### Prop rules

- Props must not imply a scientific action or product use that the script/content authority does not support.
- Vials and laboratory props are visual context, not evidence.
- Product/label text must not be invented casually by image generation.
- ID badge, orchid pin and branded objects must use approved PeptiBloom assets where available.

## 9. Expression system

The approved master provides the following labeled expression references:

- neutral;
- happy;
- curious;
- surprised;
- excited;
- thoughtful;
- explaining;
- concerned;
- focused;
- confident;
- empathetic;
- encouraging;
- questioning;
- playful;
- grateful.

These expressions define the initial allowed emotional vocabulary for Bloom.

### Expression guardrails

Expressions may vary within the canonical face, but must not:

- alter facial identity;
- become grotesque/cartoonishly distorted;
- sexualize the character;
- visually overstate scientific certainty;
- use celebration/excitement to imply efficacy or certainty beyond the approved content;
- use fear/alarm to exaggerate risk beyond the approved content.

For scientific uncertainty, prefer curious, thoughtful, focused, empathetic or concerned states over theatrical shock.

## 10. Pose and gesture system

The approved master includes Bloom in poses corresponding to:

- presenting a topic;
- explaining an idea;
- holding a vial in scientific context;
- confident standing;
- welcoming/introducing;
- interacting with Eviden.

Additional derived poses should preserve:

- open, audience-friendly body language;
- clear hand gestures;
- approachable posture;
- professional scientific/editorial context;

Avoid:

- aggressive pointing;
- dismissive gestures;
- overly theatrical influencer-style posing;
- poses that imply medical authority or clinical procedure beyond role scope;
- repeated random body-shape changes from generation drift.

## 12. Editorial behavior invariants

Bloom must consistently behave as a communicator, not as a substitute for Evidence.

She may:

- introduce a question;
- ask for clarification;
- summarize an approved explanation in simpler language;
- connect sections;
- express curiosity;
- encourage viewers to inspect evidence/limitations;
- use analogies approved by the script/content workflow.

She must not:

- invent scientific claims;
- upgrade evidence strength;
- make clinical recommendations merely because her design includes a lab coat;
- imply personal medical expertise;
- convert uncertainty into certainty for narrative simplicity;
- state unsupported efficacy/safety claims;
- treat a research vial/prop as proof of legitimacy or effectiveness.

---

## 13. Interaction with Eviden

Bloom and Eviden are complementary.

Bloom's role in duo scenes:

- frame audience questions;
- request explanation;
- translate or restate without changing evidence strength;
- guide transitions;
- provide emotional warmth and accessibility.

Eviden's role:

- provide analytical/evidence-focused explanation;
- qualify uncertainty;
- distinguish stronger from weaker evidence;
- correct oversimplification when needed.

Bloom should not visually or narratively dominate Eviden during a technical evidence qualification, and Eviden should not erase Bloom's accessibility role.

Exact scale, spacing, eyeline and blocking rules are deferred to `AV-06 Duo interaction bible`.

- blink/eye-direction variants;

- approved scene-specific prop handling;

- talking-avatar profiles;

Each derivative should retain source/version traceability where feasible.

---

## 15. Changes that require a new approved character version

A new approved version is required for material changes such as:

- permanent hairstyle redesign;
- eye-color change;
- face redesign;
- major apparent-age change;
- default wardrobe redesign;
- permanent addition/removal of signature visual identifiers;
- body/proportion redesign;
- transition from stylized illustration to a materially different visual medium if it changes character identity;
- substantial palette/role redesign.

Do not call such a change “Bloom v1.0.”

## 16. Drift QA checklist

Before approving any Bloom derivative, verify:

- [ ] face is recognizably the canonical Bloom;
- [ ] hair remains long, dark-brown, wavy and side-parted;
- [ ] brown eyes retained;
- [ ] apparent age remains consistent;
- [ ] body proportions remain consistent;
- [ ] default wardrobe/colors remain within Bloom identity when applicable;
- [ ] orchid identity elements are correct when shown;
- [ ] hands/limbs are anatomically coherent enough for production quality;
- [ ] no random logo/text hallucinations;
- [ ] no scientific implication introduced merely by props/pose;
- [ ] expression does not overstate certainty/risk;
- [ ] derivative traces back to `PB-EV-BLOOM-CHARACTER-MASTER-V1.0`;
- [ ] if identity materially changed, derivative is rejected or promoted to a new version review.
