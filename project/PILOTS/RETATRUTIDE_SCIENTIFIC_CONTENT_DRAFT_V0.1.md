## Evidence legend

- ✅ Solid: supported by peer-reviewed human evidence appropriate to the stated claim.
- ⚠ Limited/emerging: meaningful evidence exists but important scope, maturity or outcome limitations remain.
- 💬 Anecdotal: experience reports, clearly separated from scientific evidence. None are incorporated into this v0.1 draft.
- ❓ Unresolved/not established: the reviewed corpus does not support a firm conclusion.

## 1. Name

Retatrutide.

Development designation: LY3437943 / LY-3437943.

Regulatory identity: FDA GSRS UNII NOP2Y096GV. The existence of a UNII identifies a substance and does not imply regulatory approval.

## 2. Other names

- LY3437943
- LY-3437943
- Retatrutide [INN/USAN]

Editorial rule: informal terms such as “GLP-3” should not be used as a scientific classification. Retatrutide is more accurately described as a triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist.

## 3. Formula

Chemical definition for the fully modified neutral molecule in the FDA GSRS alternative chemical representation: C221H342N46O68.

Important identity note: the FDA GSRS protein record also displays an estimated backbone-only formula/molecular weight generated from the encoded peptide subunit. That estimate does not include the complete modified chemistry. For the fully modified molecule, the alternative chemical definition is the appropriate representation in this draft.

## 4. Molecular weight

Approximately 4,731.36 Da for the fully modified neutral molecule in the FDA GSRS alternative chemical definition.

Do not substitute salt-specific or vendor-specific molecular weights without identifying the exact chemical form.

## 5. Amino-acid sequence / structural modifications

Retatrutide is a modified 39-residue peptide derived from a GIP-like backbone.

FDA GSRS backbone sequence:

YAQGTFTSDYSILLDKKAQAAFIEYLLEGGPSSGAPPPS

The one-letter backbone does not capture all modifications. The full substance includes non-standard residues and lipidation, including Aib substitutions, an alpha-methyl leucine residue, a C20 fatty-diacid moiety linked through a gamma-glutamyl/AEEA linker at a lysine, and a C-terminal amide.

Because these modifications materially affect identity and pharmacokinetics, PeptiBloom should never present only the natural-letter sequence as if it were the complete chemical structure.

## 6. Family

Retatrutide is an investigational multi-receptor metabolic peptide agonist acting at:

- glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR);
- glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R);
- glucagon receptor (GCGR).

Evidence level: ✅ mechanism/identity strongly supported.

## 7. History

Early clinical development was published under the code LY3437943. A phase 1b multiple-ascending-dose study in adults with type 2 diabetes was published in 2022. Phase 2 studies in type 2 diabetes and obesity followed in 2023, with a MASLD liver-fat substudy published in 2024. In 2026, TRANSCEND-T2D-1 provided the first peer-reviewed phase 3 evidence accepted into this review.

At the evidence cutoff, retatrutide remains investigational. Phase 3 programmes continue to address obesity, type 2 diabetes, direct comparisons with established incretin therapies, cardiovascular/renal outcomes and associated conditions.

## 8. Mechanism of action

Retatrutide is a single peptide engineered to activate GIPR, GLP-1R and GCGR. Structural/mechanistic studies support direct engagement of all three receptors.

The clinical rationale is to combine incretin-mediated effects on glycaemic regulation and appetite/energy intake with glucagon-receptor pharmacology that may contribute additional metabolic effects.

Important limitation: receptor engagement and biological plausibility do not prove that any single receptor component independently causes the magnitude of weight loss observed in humans. Direct claims that GCGR activation quantitatively “burns” a defined amount of energy in humans are not supported by the core clinical evidence reviewed here.

Evidence level: ✅ receptor mechanism; ⚠ mechanistic attribution of individual clinical effects.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics

The phase 1b human study reported approximately dose-proportional pharmacokinetics and an elimination half-life of roughly 6 days.

This supports why once-weekly administration was used throughout the investigational programme.

Limitations:

- derived from early-phase human testing;
- later population-PK analyses may refine estimates;
- this is not equivalent to approved prescribing information.

Evidence level: ⚠ meaningful early human evidence.

## 10. Stability

❓ No evidence package in this review establishes a general post-reconstitution stability period for consumer-market or third-party lyophilised retatrutide vials.

Trial-supply formulation data, vendor technical sheets and handling instructions for unrelated formulations cannot be transferred automatically to another product. Stability depends on formulation, salt/form, excipients, concentration, container closure, manufacturing controls, pH, temperature, light exposure and microbiological conditions.

PeptiBloom publication rule: do not publish a specific “stable for X days after reconstitution” statement until formulation-specific analytical evidence is accepted.

## 11. Conservation

❓ No universal storage instruction for third-party retatrutide material is evidence-authorized by this review.

Commercial research suppliers publish storage recommendations for their own material, but those are product-specific technical claims and are not equivalent to validated storage instructions for an approved retatrutide medicine or for arbitrary third-party vials.

PeptiBloom should distinguish:

- investigational clinical-supply handling;
- manufacturer-specific research-material instructions;
- validated pharmaceutical stability data.

They must not be merged into one generic recommendation.

## 12. Reconstitution

❓ No PeptiBloom evidence-based reconstitution protocol is authorized in v0.1.

Retatrutide is not an approved medicine with public prescribing/reconstitution instructions at the cutoff. The reviewed clinical publications describe investigational subcutaneous administration but do not justify a general bacteriostatic-water protocol for third-party lyophilised vials.

Therefore this section should publicly state that a validated universal reconstitution procedure has not been established rather than filling the gap from vendor or community practice.

## 13. Dosage

There is no approved retatrutide dose at the evidence cutoff.

Clinical trials have studied multiple once-weekly dose levels and escalation schemes. Examples include phase 2 obesity arms ranging from 1 mg to 12 mg weekly and phase 3 TRANSCEND-T2D-1 maintenance arms of 4 mg, 9 mg and 12 mg weekly.

These numbers describe experimental trial regimens under protocol-controlled conditions. They are not PeptiBloom dosing recommendations and should never be converted directly into self-administration instructions.

Evidence level for trial regimens: ✅.
Evidence level for an approved/public-use dose: ❓ none at cutoff.

## 14. Frequency

Once-weekly subcutaneous administration is consistently used in the investigational clinical programme and is pharmacokinetically plausible given the approximately 6-day half-life observed in phase 1b.

This is a description of study administration, not an approved prescribing instruction.

## 15. Duration of treatment / “cycle”

Clinical trials have evaluated treatment over defined protocol periods, including approximately 12, 36, 40 and 48 weeks in the peer-reviewed studies reviewed here, with longer phase 3 programmes ongoing or reported through sponsor communications.

❓ A scientifically validated “cycle” for self-directed retatrutide use has not been established. The bodybuilding/research-market concept of cycling should not be inferred from clinical-trial duration.

## 16. Frequent combinations

No evidence-based “stacking” protocol is accepted in this review.

Some phase 3 studies evaluate retatrutide in participants receiving background metabolic therapies. For example, TRANSCEND-T2D-2 studies retatrutide versus semaglutide in participants receiving metformin with or without an SGLT2 inhibitor; TRANSCEND-T2D-3 studies participants with renal impairment receiving basal insulin with or without other background therapies.

These study contexts must not be transformed into recommendations to combine retatrutide with other incretin agonists or peptides.

## 17. Synergies

The molecule itself was designed around multi-receptor pharmacology: GIPR, GLP-1R and GCGR agonism within a single peptide.

That internal multi-receptor design is distinct from “stacking” retatrutide with another drug or peptide.

❓ No accepted evidence in v0.1 demonstrates that combining retatrutide with another GLP-1/GIP agonist produces a superior benefit-risk profile.

## 18. Interactions

The complete drug-drug interaction profile has not been sufficiently mapped in Evidence Review v0.1.

Particular caution is required when extrapolating from monotherapy trials to regimens containing glucose-lowering agents such as insulin because hypoglycaemia risk and dose-management questions may differ by background therapy.

The dedicated TRANSCEND-T2D-3 study is one reason this question remains open rather than inferred.

Evidence level: ❓ incomplete.

## 19. Possible adverse effects

Across the reviewed human trials, gastrointestinal events are the most consistently reported adverse effects, including nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and constipation. They were generally mild to moderate but contributed to treatment discontinuation in some participants.

Other observations requiring context include:

- dose-related/transient heart-rate increases in the phase 2 obesity study;
- one serious acute pancreatitis event in that trial, meaning uncommon pancreatitis risk cannot be ruled out;
- asymptomatic pancreatic enzyme elevations;
- altered/enhanced skin sensation or hyperesthesia reported in a minority of participants;
- treatment discontinuations due to adverse events in phase 3 TRANSCEND-T2D-1.

The absence of severe hypoglycaemia in selected monotherapy trials does not prove absence of hypoglycaemia risk in all future combinations or special populations.

Long-term rare-event safety remains incompletely characterized.

## 20. Contraindications

No approved retatrutide prescribing label exists at the cutoff; therefore PeptiBloom should not invent an official contraindication list.

Evidence gaps remain particularly important for pregnancy/lactation, paediatric use, some severe comorbidities and combination regimens.

The scientifically correct public representation is “not established / investigational” rather than copying contraindications from another incretin medicine as though they automatically apply identically.

## 21. Parameters for monitoring

A PeptiBloom clinical-monitoring protocol is not authorized by this Evidence Review.

Clinical trials measure parameters including body weight, HbA1c/glucose, adverse events, vital signs and laboratory markers relevant to study objectives. This demonstrates what researchers measured; it does not establish a universal monitoring prescription for unsupervised use.

Future publication may summarize trial monitoring domains if Evidence approves the exact representation.

## 22. Frequently asked questions

### Is retatrutide approved?
No at the evidence cutoff. It remains investigational.

### Is retatrutide simply a “stronger GLP-1”?
That description is incomplete. It is a single triple agonist of GIPR, GLP-1R and GCGR.

### Has it produced large weight reductions in trials?
Yes. In the peer-reviewed phase 2 obesity trial, the 12 mg study arm showed a mean 24.2% reduction from baseline at 48 weeks, versus 2.1% with placebo. This is a trial result, not a guarantee for an individual.

### Is it proven better than tirzepatide?
Not at the cutoff. TRIUMPH-5 is the direct phase 3 comparison and results are not yet available for a conclusion in this review.

### Is it proven better than semaglutide for T2D?
Not at the cutoff. TRANSCEND-T2D-2 directly addresses that question and has not yielded accepted comparative results in this review.

### Does it reverse fatty liver disease?
It has produced very large MRI-measured reductions in liver fat in a small phase 2a MASLD substudy. That is not the same as proving fibrosis reversal, prevention of cirrhosis or reduced liver-related mortality.

### Can PeptiBloom provide a reconstitution schedule?
Not from the current evidence. A universal third-party-vial protocol is not validated.

### Is long-term cardiovascular protection proven?
No. TRIUMPH-Outcomes remains ongoing with no results posted at the evidence cutoff.

## 23. Most common errors in interpreting retatrutide

1. Calling it “GLP-3” as though that were a receptor or accepted pharmacologic class.
2. Treating trial doses as approved doses.
3. Quoting sponsor topline phase 3 results without identifying the source class.
4. Assuming weight-loss magnitude from one population/duration applies to everyone.
5. Equating liver-fat reduction with proven fibrosis reversal or liver-event prevention.
6. Treating improvements in risk markers as proof of cardiovascular-event reduction.
7. Claiming superiority over tirzepatide before the direct head-to-head trial reports.
8. Copying reconstitution/storage directions from research vendors and labeling them clinical evidence.
9. Treating absence of a rare adverse event in a modest trial as proof that the risk does not exist.
10. Presenting preclinical mechanistic findings as demonstrated human therapeutic outcomes.

## 24. Scientific evidence level

### Overall PeptiBloom assessment at cutoff

**Human efficacy evidence: high for short-to-medium-term glycaemic and weight outcomes within studied populations.**

Supporting basis:
- randomized phase 1b/2 human studies;
- peer-reviewed phase 2 obesity and T2D trials;
- peer-reviewed phase 3 T2D trial.

**Long-term outcomes evidence: incomplete.**

Unresolved areas include cardiovascular/renal event reduction, rare long-term safety and durability after discontinuation.

**MASLD evidence: promising but limited.**

Large liver-fat reductions are supported by a small imaging substudy; hard liver outcomes are not established.

**Regulatory/approved-use evidence: none.**

Retatrutide remains investigational at the cutoff.

**Third-party reconstitution/stability evidence: insufficient for PeptiBloom instructions.**

## Recomendación PeptiBloom — draft evidence interpretation

PeptiBloom does **not** recommend a dose, cycle, reconstitution protocol or self-directed use of retatrutide on the basis of the current evidence.

The evidence-supported conclusion is narrower and more useful:

Retatrutide is one of the most clinically advanced investigational triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonists. Randomized human trials demonstrate substantial effects on body weight and glycaemic control in the populations studied, and peer-reviewed phase 3 T2D evidence is now available. At the same time, the compound remains investigational; direct comparative superiority against tirzepatide and semaglutide is unresolved at the cutoff; cardiovascular and renal outcome trials are incomplete; long-term rare-event safety remains under characterization; and no approved public-use dose, reconstitution procedure or third-party-vial stability specification exists.

### Reasoning chain

1. **Biological plausibility:** strong receptor-level evidence supports genuine triple agonism.
2. **Human efficacy:** randomized trials repeatedly show large glycaemic and/or weight effects, reducing dependence on mechanistic inference.
3. **Evidence maturity:** phase 3 T2D publication raises confidence for that specific population/outcome, but phase 3 obesity evidence is not yet fully peer-reviewed in the accepted corpus.
4. **Safety:** common GI effects are consistent across trials, while uncommon/long-term risks remain insufficiently characterized.
5. **Comparative efficacy:** ongoing direct comparator trials prevent scientifically defensible claims of superiority today.
6. **Regulatory status:** investigational status means trial regimens cannot be reframed as approved dosing.
7. **Preparation/stability:** absence of formulation-specific validated evidence blocks PeptiBloom from inventing reconstitution and storage protocols.
8. **Conclusion:** scientifically promising and supported for defined trial outcomes; not mature enough for PeptiBloom to turn the evidence into an unsupervised administration protocol.

This section must undergo scientific recheck before publication.
