# PB-SCI-PILOT-001 — Retatrutide Scientific Library Pilot

Status: PROPOSED PILOT

## Purpose

Use one compound to validate the complete PeptiBloom scientific-publication workflow before scaling production across the library.

## Why Retatrutide

Retatrutide is proposed as the first pilot because it is scientifically active, clinically developed, heavily discussed publicly and likely to exercise the difficult parts of the PeptiBloom model: mechanism, human clinical evidence, uncertainty, safety, dosing claims, manufacturer/development context, emerging evidence and anecdotal discussion.

This pilot selection does not imply endorsement, clinical recommendation, commercial priority or readiness for public sale.

## Pilot objective

Demonstrate an auditable path from source discovery to approved public content:

`Scientific Inventory -> Evidence review -> Evidence-qualified claim packages -> Scientific Content -> Scientific recheck -> Editorial review -> Owner publication approval -> Web representation`

## Required research scope

The pilot should investigate, where evidence exists:

1. Name and recognized alternate names.
2. Molecular/formula information and sequence where applicable and verifiable.
3. Compound family/class.
4. Development history.
5. Mechanism of action.
6. Pharmacokinetics.
7. Human clinical evidence.
8. Preclinical evidence where relevant and clearly separated.
9. Safety and adverse events.
10. Contraindications or populations not established, using precise wording.
11. Drug/compound interactions where evidence exists.
12. Stability and storage, distinguishing published/technical evidence from vendor claims.
13. Reconstitution evidence, if any legitimate technical evidence exists; absence of verified evidence must remain explicit.
14. Dosing regimens actually studied in trials, clearly distinguished from recommendations.
15. Treatment duration studied.
16. Evidence for combinations/synergies, if any.
17. Monitoring parameters used or supported in relevant clinical research.
18. Active/registered clinical trials and development status at the evidence cutoff date.
19. Regulatory status at the evidence cutoff date.
20. Important evidence gaps and controversies.
21. Anecdotal/community claims, only as a separately labeled observational layer when useful for misinformation analysis or user-context understanding.

## Required source hierarchy

Priority should be given to:

- peer-reviewed primary studies;
- trial registries and posted results;
- primary regulatory documents;
- patents where relevant to history/formulation/mechanism;
- technical/manufacturer documents only for clearly attributed technical/development facts;
- systematic reviews/meta-analyses for synthesis, without double-counting underlying studies;
- community sources only as anecdotal evidence and never as proof of efficacy or safety.

## PeptiBloom article structure under test

The pilot should test whether the publication model can support the following article sections without forcing unsupported content:

1. Nombre
2. Otros nombres
3. Fórmula
4. Peso molecular
5. Secuencia de aminoácidos (si aplica)
6. Familia
7. Historia
8. Mecanismo de acción
9. Farmacocinética
10. Estabilidad
11. Conservación
12. Reconstitución
13. Dosificación — studied regimens vs recommendations must remain distinct
14. Frecuencia
15. Duración estudiada / ciclo — avoid implying unsupported cycling protocols
16. Combinaciones frecuentes — evidence vs anecdote must remain explicit
17. Sinergias
18. Interacciones
19. Posibles efectos adversos
20. Contraindicaciones / poblaciones no establecidas
21. Parámetros de seguimiento usados o razonables only if Evidence supports wording
22. Preguntas frecuentes
23. Errores frecuentes / misconceptions
24. Nivel de evidencia científica
25. Referencias bibliográficas completas
26. Recomendación PeptiBloom / interpretación razonada, only after defining the allowed meaning and ensuring it cannot be confused with individualized medical advice or unsupported clinical instruction

## Important pilot constraint: “Recomendación PeptiBloom”

The pilot must explicitly resolve the semantics of this section before publication.

It may summarize the evidence-weighted PeptiBloom interpretation of the literature, including what is known, uncertain and not established. It must not become a disguised prescribing protocol, individualized medical recommendation or commercial endorsement.

If safe/publication-compatible wording cannot be defined, the section remains blocked rather than being filled editorially.

## Visual-content test

The pilot should also generate a publication-safe visual summary covering, only where supported:

- what it is;
- mechanism;
- studied administration/reconstitution information where verifiable;
- studied dose ranges rather than invented dosing advice;
- studied frequency/duration;
- storage/stability evidence;
- combinations only with evidence label;
- precautions;
- evidence level;
- PeptiBloom evidence interpretation.

The infographic must not contain stronger claims than the reviewed long-form article.

## Quality-control checklist

Before pilot approval:

- verify every numeric value against its source;
- verify mg/mcg and concentration conversions if present;
- verify any syringe-unit conversion separately and exclude it unless publication policy explicitly permits it and the underlying use is scientifically justified;
- verify trial dose units, schedules and durations;
- verify formula, molecular weight and sequence against authoritative sources;
- verify clinical-trial identifiers and statuses at the evidence cutoff date;
- verify regulatory-status statements from current primary sources;
- verify storage/stability statements and distinguish drug substance, formulated investigational product and vendor vial claims;
- verify every citation supports the adjacent claim;
- mark all unverified data explicitly rather than infer it.

## Pilot success criteria

The pilot succeeds only if:

1. the Evidence -> Scientific Content contract works without requiring scientific invention;
2. the Scientific Content -> Publication contract produces an auditable approved revision;
3. unresolved evidence gaps can remain visible without breaking the page design;
4. references/provenance remain inspectable;
5. the article and infographic preserve the same evidence level and uncertainty;
6. Web can render the approved representation without becoming the source of scientific truth;
7. the process is repeatable for subsequent compounds.

## Pilot non-goals

- no ecommerce;
- no supplier selection;
- no commercial product claims;
- no protocol copied from vendors;
- no individualized medical advice;
- no assumption that commonly discussed online regimens are scientifically supported;
- no automatic publication.

## Proposed next action

Begin source discovery and evidence mapping for Retatrutide. Do not draft the final public article first. Build the reviewed evidence/claim map, identify gaps and contradictions, then transform only qualified material into the publication draft.
